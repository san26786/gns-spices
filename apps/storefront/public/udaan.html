Legally, ethically, structurally

The four pillars that prove GNS is pure FMCG commerce.

Not compliance theatre — four specific structural rules. Break any one of them and this would become an MLM. All four holding is why it isn't.

01

Zero recruitment rewards

Strict legal & ethical compliance

In an MLM or pyramid scheme, a participant earns money simply by recruiting another person. In GNS UDAAN and UDYAM: you earn ₹0 for signing up 10, 100 or 1,000 people. Money is disbursed only when a physical product is sold to and consumed by a retail customer or Kirana shop.

02

Everyday FMCG price integrity

MLMs rely on inflated pricing because their commissions require massive artificial margins. GNS operates on everyday essential food products where consumers buy based on genuine kitchen need and price fairness. Sales are driven by organic consumer pull — not speculative earnings promises.

03

Decoupled architecture — no stock-loading

One of the most predatory aspects of MLM is forcing individuals to buy inventory to “qualify” for commission tiers. GNS separates supply from demand entirely: UDAAN advocates carry ZERO inventory — they never buy, store or ship stock. UDYAM District Distributors handle 100% of the inventory in audited warehouses. Advocate creates demand → district distributor delivers the physical goods.

04

Non-pipelining governance

In traditional MLMs, missing or inactive tiers trickle commissions up to top-tier “gurus” as windfall profits — creating a top-heavy pyramid. Under the GNS 70:30 model, margins are strictly role-based. If an intermediate layer isn't present in a transaction (no regional partner, no advocate), those unallocated funds do not become middleman windfalls. They revert into the U6 / A5 Performance Growth Pools to fund performance bonuses and retail promotions.
